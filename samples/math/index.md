# Math

Formulas for the shapes that appear in the [geometry quiz](geometry.quiz.md).

| Shape | Area | Perimeter | Volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Square, side $s$ | $s^2$ | $4s$ | — |
| Rectangle, $l \times w$ | $lw$ | $2(l + w)$ | — |
| Triangle, base $b$ and height $h$ | $\frac{1}{2}bh$ | $a + b + c$ | — |
| Circle, radius $r$ | $\pi r^2$ | $2\pi r$ | — |
| Sphere, radius $r$ | $4\pi r^2$ (surface) | — | $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |
| Cylinder, radius $r$ and height $h$ | $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$ (surface) | — | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Cone, radius $r$ and slant $l$ | $\pi r^2 + \pi rl$ (surface) | — | $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |
| Cube, side $s$ | $6s^2$ | $12s$ (edges) | $s^3$ |

<details>
<summary>Worked example: a circle with radius 3</summary>

The area squares the radius, the circumference doubles it:

$$
A = \pi r^2 = \pi \cdot 3^2 = 9\pi \approx 28.27
$$

$$
C = 2\pi r = 2\pi \cdot 3 = 6\pi \approx 18.85
$$

Squaring grows faster than doubling, so the area is always the larger number once $r > 2$.
The two formulas are the most common thing to swap in an exam.

</details>

<summary>Remember: the angles of a triangle</summary>

The three interior angles always total $180^\circ$, so the missing angle is

$$
180^\circ - 45^\circ - 65^\circ = 70^\circ
$$
